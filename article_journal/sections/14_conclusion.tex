\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \mra{}, a reproducible benchmark and evaluation framework
for onboard-only autonomous underwater gate racing. Its central contribution is
an enforced separation between autonomy and evaluation: controllers use only
allow-listed onboard sensing and physically received messages, while an
independent referee uses privileged state to adjudicate ordered crossings,
penalties and completion.

The experiments show that the contract is usable across heterogeneous circuits,
currents, fleets and controller types. The reference controller completes the
current-free demonstration, while the systematic matrix exposes
condition-dependent controller robustness and coordination failures that clean
single-vehicle scores would miss. Learned policies integrate under the same
contract and provide a useful diagnostic: local gate transitions can be learned,
but their reliability currently degrades when transitions must be chained over
full circuits. This result strengthens the benchmark's purpose without making
learning a second central contribution.

The evidence remains simulation-based and bounded by one backend, a narrow
current sweep, two rule-controller families, a small fleet and an exploratory
pose extension that is not used by the main controllers. Learned results are a
retrospective diagnostic rather than a held-out generalization claim. Future
work should extend the disturbance and fleet axes, add sequence-aware learned
controllers, seal new evaluation circuits and transfer the contract to a
physical BlueROV2. In each case, \mra{} provides the instrument for making the
comparison reproducible and meaningful.
