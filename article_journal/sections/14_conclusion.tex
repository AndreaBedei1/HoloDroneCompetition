\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \mra{}, a configurable and reproducible benchmark and
evaluation framework for autonomous underwater gate racing. The framework
separates participant autonomy from race adjudication through a
controller-agnostic participant interface, a configurable race-management layer
and an independent referee. Controllers operate within a common
participant-level information boundary and provide body-frame commands, while
privileged simulator state is reserved for ordered-crossing validation,
penalties and official race results.

The framework was validated with two rule-based reference controllers and a
recurrent PPO controller, demonstrating that autonomy methods with substantially
different internal designs can be integrated through the same benchmark
interface. Experiments across heterogeneous circuits, a medium-current
condition and multi-vehicle configurations showed that the benchmark can expose
differences in passage strategy, disturbance robustness and vehicle interaction.
The fleet experiments further demonstrated that the same evaluation framework
can represent both independent multi-vehicle operation and distributed
cooperative behaviour while preserving separate referee state for each
participant.

Future work will extend these capabilities along several complementary
directions. Multi-vehicle evaluation can incorporate more advanced cooperation
strategies, particularly under environmental disturbances where vehicle
interaction, waiting policies and shared progress information become more
challenging. Competitive multi-team scenarios and heterogeneous vehicle fleets
with different dynamics and sensing suites can also be evaluated under the same
race-management and referee framework. Current-aware control and explicit
disturbance compensation provide another natural extension, together with a
broader evaluation over different current magnitudes, directions and
time-varying flow conditions. On the learning side, the recurrent PPO controller
serves as a baseline demonstration and can be replaced or complemented by more
advanced reinforcement-learning, recurrent or attention-based approaches.
Finally, transferring the benchmark interface and race protocol to physical
underwater vehicles will enable direct investigation of sim-to-real behaviour
and real-world racing experiments.
