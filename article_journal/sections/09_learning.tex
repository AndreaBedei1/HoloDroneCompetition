\section{Learning-Based Controller}
\label{sec:learning}

Learning is included as a supporting demonstration that a policy learned from
interaction can be integrated into \mra{} through the same participant-level
information boundary and action interface used by the reference controllers.
The benchmark itself is unchanged: observations are constructed from
participant-accessible information, actions use the same four body-frame command
channels and performance is assessed independently by the referee.

\subsection{Observation and Action Representation}
\label{sec:learning_obs}

The learned controller receives a 27-dimensional observation vector constructed
exclusively from information available to the participant. The representation
combines acoustic information about the locally tracked beacon, visual
information about the associated gate target, vehicle motion and attitude
measurements, the previous control action and short-term temporal changes in the
observed target.

More specifically, the observation includes beacon availability, acoustic
bearing, elevation and range, visual target centre and apparent size, depth,
body-frame DVL velocity, yaw-rate information, the previous surge, sway, heave
and yaw commands, short-term variations of the visual and acoustic measurements,
a target-transition indicator and, when available, a local estimate of gate
orientation. Availability indicators distinguish valid measurements from missing
ones, while unavailable quantities are represented with neutral values.

No global vehicle pose, exact gate coordinates, future course geometry, referee
progress or referee-derived event information is included. The representation is
therefore a local observation of the current vehicle--gate interaction rather
than a privileged representation of the course state.

The policy outputs the same four normalized continuous commands used by the
reference controllers,
\[
u_t =
\left[
u_t^{\mathrm{surge}},
u_t^{\mathrm{sway}},
u_t^{\mathrm{heave}},
u_t^{\mathrm{yaw}}
\right],
\qquad
u_t\in[-1,1]^4,
\]
which are subsequently handled by the common vehicle adapter and safety
constraints.

\subsection{Recurrent PPO Controller}
\label{sec:learning_policy}

The learning-based controller uses Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO)~\cite{schulman2017ppo} with a recurrent actor--critic policy. Recurrence is
used because a single observation does not fully describe the state of an
underwater gate passage: visual detections may disappear near the aperture,
acoustic measurements vary over time and successful passage verification depends
on the recent evolution of the vehicle and target.

The policy first encodes the current observation into a compact latent
representation. A recurrent memory then combines this representation with
information retained from previous control steps. Separate actor and critic
branches use this temporal representation to produce, respectively, the
continuous four-dimensional control command and the value estimate required
during training.

This structure allows the learned controller to exploit temporal information
without receiving explicit course progress from the referee. At execution time,
the policy maintains only its internal recurrent state and the participant-level
observations supplied by the benchmark.

\subsection{Training Procedure}
\label{sec:learning_training}

Training is organized progressively rather than exposing the policy immediately
to the complete racing task. Early stages emphasize the local behaviours required
to approach, align with and traverse a gate, while subsequent training exposes
the controller to longer sequences and complete circuits. The final training
stage uses the complete official circuits under the same observation and action
interfaces used during evaluation.

The reward combines task progress with control objectives that encourage
effective gate traversal. Positive feedback is associated with successful gate
crossings, course completion, forward progress and alignment, while elapsed time,
collisions and terminal failures are penalized. The reward is used only during
training and is never part of the participant observation presented to the
policy.

Training produces a sequence of policy checkpoints. A single checkpoint is
selected and frozen before the final evaluation, after which no further learning
or adaptation is performed. This separation ensures that the experiments of
Section~\ref{sec:eval_rq5} evaluate a fixed controller rather than a policy that
continues to change during testing.

The official circuits were available during the development and training process.
The resulting experiments should therefore be interpreted as an evaluation of
learning-based control and benchmark integration on the defined race scenarios,
rather than as a claim of generalization to previously unseen courses.
